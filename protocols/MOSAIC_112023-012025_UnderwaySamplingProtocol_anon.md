## BACKGROUND

### Summary

### Method description and rationale
eDNA from NOAA-CalCOFI Ocean Genomics (NCOG) samples only yielded 12% success in marine mammal detection and mainly showed the presence of delphinids despite visual observations of whales at several of the sampled stations. This low detection rate may be due to the small volume of water filtered through PES-sterivex 0.2 um cartridges, the high ratio of microbial:eukaryotic DNA, the DNA processing optimized for microbiome studies, or a combination of all of the above. In this study, we have designed a new sampling protocol which incorporates the following components: filtering a larger volume of water, using a larger pore size MCE filter to exclude microbial DNA and capture more eDNA from larger organisms (fish and marine mammals), and processing water from the underway system to cover a larger area of the surface ocean. The sampling will also benefit from communication with the marine mammal observers on deck during transit. 

### Spatial coverage and environment(s) of relevance

This protocol can be used on any oceanographic research vessel with an underway pump that delivers surface seawater to the lab and a CTD.

## PERSONNEL REQUIRED

One person with pipetting experience. Research vessel experience is recommended but not required. Two people can sample 24/7.

### Safety

There are no major safety concerns with this protocol. Proper PPE should be worn at all times, especially when sterilizing equipment with bleach or sampling from a CTD on deck.

### Training requirements

Standard molecular biology training including sterile technique and pipetting technique is required to properly conduct this protocol. Research vessel experience is recommended. In-person training in this method prior to the cruise is recommended.

### Time needed to execute the procedure

The process of setting up, starting, and finishing a sample should take around 60 minutes. The time to actually sample is determined by the goals of the study, since the samples are left running. Samples must be checked on approximately every 20 minutes during the sampling.

## STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURE

### Notes

- You will be sampling during the day in parallel with deck observers. Collaborate as much as possible with the observers so you can keep track of mammal sightings. 
- On each DATE, one or more TRANSECTS will be sampled. Each transect starts and ends at a STATION. 
- The UNDERWAY system pulls water directly from the surface of the ocean (~5m deep). It operates consistently, but you can control the outflow through the tap at your work station. You should aim to run the tap and filter water from the beginning to the end of a transect ONLY WHILE IN TRANSIT. Turn off the tap while the ship is on station. See below for full protocol.
- To ensure you have time to filter enough water, only filter from the underway system on transects that will last AT LEAST 30 MIN. The transect and station information will be written on the white board in the main lab; check this board at the beginning of each transect and consult with a CalCOFI technician if you have questions.
- At every STATION, there will be a CTD CAST. This cast will capture water in Niskin bottles from multiple depths. If your schedule allows, you should sample water from bottles taken at different depths, mix these samples, and filter them in the same way as you are filtering the underway water. See below for full protocol.

### Sampling Protocol

#### Underway water filtration

Goal: filter at least 5 L water SLOWLY and CONTINUOUSLY over the course of a transect. If possible, filter 12-15L.
- After the ship begins transiting, turn on the underway outflow tap. Allow the water to run for 5 minutes to flush out any debris in the line. you can run the line while you set up your filter.
- Turn off the tap and attach the pump tubing to the tap outflow.
- Place the tubing outflow end in a carboy so you can collect the outflow.
- Turn on the tap. 
- If you can regulate the outflow JUST WITH THE TAP, you don’t need to use the peristaltic pump! But if it is only an on/off setting, or if the control is not fine-scale, run the tubing through the peristaltic pump head and close the head, ensuring the tubing is not pinched.
- Turn off the tap. Discard any filtrate collected in the carboy.
- Attach the Swinnex housing + filter to the adapter at the end of the tubing and use a 20 L carboy to catch the outflow.
- Turn on the tap and adjust the outflow with a) just the tap or b) the tap and pump speed. MARK THE START TIME.
- Measure the outflow volume using the graduated cylinder after 15 minutes and calculate how many liters will get filtered at that rate for the entire transect. If more than 20 L, decrease the flow rate to prevent filter clogging. If less than 5 L, increase the flow rate to ensure enough volume.
- If you notice a big decrease in flow rate through the filter indicating that water is no longer passing through, this means the filter is likely CLOGGED. If no water passes through after ~10 minutes of continuous flow, you can end the filtration and process the filter (see below). 
  - If the filter clogs near the end of a transect, there is no need to start a second filter. However, if it is less than halfway through the transect, attach a NEW FILTER to the line and continue filtration. Make a note in the sample sheet that >1 filter was used for this transect.
- At the end of the transect, end the filtration by turning off the tap (and pump, if running). 
- Measure the FILTRATE VOLUME and MARK THE END TIME. This is very important!
- On a clean, dry work bench, set out your forceps decontamination kit: 4 x 50 mL Falcon tubes containing DNAway, water (x 2 tubes), and EtOH, in that order.
- Detach the Swinnex housing + filter from the tubing. On your work space, disassemble the Swinnex and transfer the filter into a labeled cryovial. DECONTAMINATE your forceps by dipping them sequentially into the Falcon tubes before touching the filter. Label the tube - see below for the labeling scheme. 
  - Do your best to roll the filter into a little tube and stick into the vial. It is helpful to use two sets of forceps for this.
- Place the cryovial in a -80°C freezer as soon as possible.

#### CTD water filtration

- There may be extra water available from the CTD cast, including from bottles taken at different depths. If this happens during your shift and you have the time and ability to do so, you can sample this extra water for a direct comparison with the underway samples.
- Check with the CalCOFI technicians which bottle numbers have extra water available. 
- Sample 1 L water from 3-5 bottles (taken from DIFFERENT DEPTHS) each, as allowed by availability. 
- From each bottle: open the valve at the bottom of the Niskin bottle. Allow water to flow for 5-10 seconds and use the flow through to rinse your collection bottle 3x. without collecting it. Place your collection container under the flow and collect ~2 L. Close the valve, move to the next bottle, repeat.
- Record the bottle numbers and volume sampled from each bottle.
- Mix the sample container by inverting 5-10 times.
  - If the container is too heavy to invert, just tilt it back and forth so the water sloshes and mixes inside.
- Take the sample into the lab and use it as the source water for your filter (instead of the underway tap). Perform the filtration and store the filter as described above (you will need to run the line through the peristaltic pump).
- Between CTDs (for instance, while you are filtering the underway) rinse your collection container THREE TIMES with milliQ water.

#### Sterilization
1. Prepare Cleaning Solutions: Using 4L sterile Whirlpak bags (or similar) fill one with a 20% bleach solution, and one with milliQ water. Place each Whirlpak into a jug to hold it upright. 
2. Set up Peristaltic Pump and Outflow - Set up and plug in pump with two heads. Each head will have one tube running through it. Set the pump direction in the direction of the outflow (i.e. into a sink). In the adjacent sink where the outflow is to flow, place two measuring devices that can stand on own and that can measure up to ~3L
3. Assemble Equipment to be Cleaned (See Figure 2): Attach a used filter housing to a used rubber tube if not already attached. Remove the cotton piece from the mouth of a sterile 10 mL serological pipet and fit the inflow tubing end over it. Open the Whirl Pak bag containing bleach and place the serological pipet into it. The pipet should reach nearly to the bottom of the bag without the tubing touching or entering the bag interior. Secure the pipet by wrapping the Whirl Pak opening around it and twisting the ties. On the outflow side, use a small, clear rubber tube to fit over the outflow on the filter housing and direct it into the measuring device in the sink. Fit the tube into the pump head and secure it. Do this entire step twice, once for each tube.
4. Pump Bleach and MilliQ: Turn the pump on and pump ~500mL of bleach through each tube, then switch the tube over to the milliQ and pump ~2L of milliQ through the same tube to rinse. Repeat this step for each tube.
5. Dry equipment (See Figure 3): Dry the filter housings and tubing in a fumigated hood if possible. Once dry, use UV light to further disinfect. Store equipment in sterile Whirlpak bags or similar. 

#### Negative Controls

To take a negative control, start with a filter in a housing. Fill a Whirl-Pak with 2 L DI water and run it through the filter with the peristaltic pump. Once the 2 liters have flowed through the housing, take the housing to the sink where the underway samples are taken and rub the housing around the mouths of the outflow carboys used for underway samples. Put the housing in the PCR hood and process the filter as normal.

#### Data records
- While filtering, record all the relevant information on the printed copy of the appropriate data sheet (underway vs Niskin samples).
- Periodically (at least once per day) transcribe the written records into the electronic copy of the sample sheet.
- Underway samples should be labeled as follows: 
\<CRUISE-ID>_ \<LINENUMBER-TRANSECTNUMBER>_\<FILTER-NUMBER>
  - For example: RS2311_LINE80-01_F001
- Niskin samples should be labeled as follows:
\<CRUISE-ID>\_\<STATION>\_\<CTD-CAST>_\<FILTER-NUMBER>
  - For example: RS2311_STATIONX_C001_F002
